# Motion craft

## Visual authorship

Choose one concept and let it organize the film. Build visual hierarchy before adding effects. Every shot should make the message clearer, advance the story, or create a deliberate emotional beat.

## Timing and movement

- Choose duration and easing according to distance, mass, emphasis, and reading time.
- Give movement a cause: reveal, focus, comparison, connection, emphasis, or transition.
- Separate lead, supporting motion, and resting elements. Not every object should move at once.
- Use anticipation and follow-through only when they support the subject; settle important elements cleanly.
- Build transitions from the visual idea—shape change, matching direction, masking, spatial continuation, or an intentional cut.
- Avoid defaulting to bounce, glow, particles, camera shake, generic zooms, or continuous drift.

## Production discipline

Review the still composition first, then entrances, exits, transitions, and the complete rhythm at normal speed. Motion blur and 3D are tools, not quality guarantees. Keep frame-driven video deterministic and seekable.
